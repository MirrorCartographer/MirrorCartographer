01

Observation is not explanation

A scan, blood marker, symptom, or wearable trace is a measurement with a method, time, and error range. It does not explain its own cause.

02

Diagnosis is not mechanism

ALS and MS are clinically meaningful categories. Within each, biological pathways and treatment responses can differ.

03

Prediction is not repair

Even a perfect model cannot regenerate a neuron or remyelinate an axon without an intervention capable of doing so.

The sensor stack

Different instruments see different layers.

  • MRI: anatomy and lesions
  • EMG/NCS: electrical and peripheral motor-unit evidence
  • Blood/CSF: selected molecular and immune signals
  • OCT/visual tests: retinal and optic pathway measures
  • Wearables: activity, sleep, heart rate, posture
  • Clinical exam: function, reflexes, strength, sensation

No single channel is a complete nervous-system readout. Each has blind spots, resolution limits, and context requirements.

ALS is not one molecular pathway.

Genetic, protein-homeostasis, RNA-processing, axonal transport, mitochondrial, and glial mechanisms can contribute in different proportions. A model must preserve heterogeneity.

Hard safety boundary

AI proposes; accountable care decides.

Real neurological interventions require evidence, contraindication review, informed consent, monitoring, and qualified clinical oversight. A system that can alter biology must be more constrained—not less—than a system that only makes predictions.

Room 06 / the dark matter

What the model cannot see is part of the model.

Unmeasured variables are not zero. Missing data is not normal data. Unknown is a first-class state, not an invitation to invent.

?

Measurement limits

Spatial resolution, temporal sampling, tissue access, assay sensitivity, calibration drift, and noisy proxies.

∅

Causal ambiguity

Correlation may reflect cause, consequence, compensation, shared upstream factors, or selection bias.

↗

Intervention limits

Target engagement may fail; delivery may be inadequate; a beneficial pathway may have harmful effects elsewhere.

Failure modes the system must expose

  • Confident prediction from sparse or out-of-distribution data.
  • Training labels that encode diagnostic delay, unequal access, or historical bias.
  • Biomarker change mistaken for meaningful functional benefit.
  • Short-term improvement that hides long-term harm.
  • Digital twin drift as the real biology changes.
  • Model-generated mechanisms that cannot be independently reproduced.
Room 07 / the secret in C

Every conclusion must show its ancestry.

The repository's provenance discipline is the central instrument: distinguish source facts, observations, hypotheses, interpretations, decisions, and unknowns. A beautiful model without lineage is an unreliable instrument.

The unmeasured is not empty.

This room is a conceptual research prototype. The synthetic scanner, state vector, counterfactual simulator, and ledger are educational demonstrations. They do not contain a validated neurological model, patient data, real-time sensing, or treatment efficacy estimates.

Evidence maturity: a 2026 systematic scoping review identified 36 studies of neurological digital twins; most were proof-of-concept or retrospective, with limited external validation and no randomized controlled trials identified. Digital-twin language here describes a research architecture, not a validated patient-specific simulator.

Regulatory boundary: FDA's January 2026 clinical decision-support guidance distinguishes reference/decision-support functions from software functions that analyze medical images or signals, or provide specific patient-level diagnostic or treatment directives. This prototype is not intended for clinical use. Any future patient-data, signal-analysis, or recommendation capability requires product-specific regulatory and clinical review.
