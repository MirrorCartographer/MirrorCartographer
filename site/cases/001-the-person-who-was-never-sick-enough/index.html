05 / Competing interpretations

Three explanations. None gets a free pass.

HYPOTHESIS A

Reasonable narrow decisions

Each professional answered a limited question correctly. The overall gap arose because no one owned longitudinal synthesis.

Would support: clear scope limits, appropriate handoffs, no shared synthesis responsibility.

Would weaken: evidence that a responsible reviewer received and ignored the full trajectory.

HYPOTHESIS B

Information transmission failure

The functional follow-up existed but did not enter the packet used for the decision. The failure is in transfer, indexing, or submission.

Would support: R-05 transmission receipt missing; packet manifest excludes it.

Would weaken: confirmed receipt and documented consideration of R-05.

HYPOTHESIS C

Evidence rule mismatch

The packet may have contained the relevant facts, but the fictional program's threshold did not recognize the type of functional evidence provided.

Would support: written rule requiring a specific measurement not collected.

Would weaken: rule explicitly accepts the evidence submitted.

06 / Provenance & limits

Nothing here gets to pretend it came from the real world.

Source register

Origin: authored synthetic records, created solely for this case.

External sources: none. No real patient file, clinical guideline, insurer policy, or institution was used.

Transformations: chronology, contradiction ledger, and interactive summary are derived only from the six fictional records above.

Known unknowns

Actual screening value; symptom frequency and severity; functional baseline; submission manifest; governing evidence rule; whether the closure could be appealed; subsequent outcome.

These are intentionally unresolved. Filling them with invented detail would make the case less honest.

What this case demonstrates: a summary can materially change when a relevant record is omitted. It does not demonstrate how frequently this happens in real healthcare, prove misconduct, or establish that any particular decision was wrong.
