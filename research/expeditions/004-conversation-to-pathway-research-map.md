## 0. Scope and honesty contract

This artifact reconstructs the conversation from the current conversation, available recent-conversation summaries, and established MC project context. It does **not** claim that every historical chat message or every repository file has been exhaustively retrieved. Where a turn is represented by a summary rather than its raw text, the reconstruction is marked as such.

This is a map of inquiry, not a claim that every branch has been researched to completion. It distinguishes:
- **Observed conversation move:** what the user asked, challenged, corrected, or connected.
- **Interpretive reading:** what problem the move may be pointing toward.
- **Research question:** what can be investigated externally.
- **Hypothesis:** a provisional explanation that could be wrong.
- **Evidence:** source-backed finding, with scope and limitations.
- **Symbolic layer:** meaningful metaphor or image, not scientific evidence.
- **Product implication:** what MC should preserve, reveal, test, or help navigate.
- **Proof state:** what has actually been checked, built, tested, or remains missing.

No model-generated perspective is represented as an independent human expert review. “Research minds” below are explicit intellectual roles used to generate and challenge interpretations. Their conclusions remain subject to source checking and domain-expert review.

### Sequence-level interpretation

The conversation repeatedly moves from an observable event to the hidden coordination that makes it possible; from an apparent failure to the process that normally prevents or resolves it; from a part to the integrity of the whole; and from a metaphor to a request for a testable distinction.

The strongest stable research question is therefore:

**How do living systems detect changing conditions, coordinate activity across levels, decide what to preserve or remove, contain local damage, restore function, and update their behavior over time—and how can we infer those processes when only partial signals are observable?**

This is a research-program question, not a discovered universal law.

## 2. The research minds: explicit roles and their operating questions

These roles are deliberately different. They should disagree when their disciplines imply different standards or explanations.

| Research mind | Primary lens | What it asks at every node | Typical overreach it must prevent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanistic biologist | Molecular and cellular mechanisms | What physical process produces the observation? | Treating a metaphor as a mechanism |
| Systems biologist | Interactions across nested levels | What changes when components interact? | Assuming the whole is more than an unmeasured slogan |
| Control theorist / cyberneticist | Feedback, delay, stability, control | What is sensed, what is controlled, what is delayed, and what closes the loop? | Calling every regulation process feedback without specifying the loop |
| Physiologist | Function under changing conditions | What does the system do, and under what load or state? | Equating activation with benefit |
| Immunologist | Recognition, tolerance, inflammation, resolution | What initiates, sustains, terminates, or redirects the response? | Describing the immune system as either inherently protective or inherently harmful |
| Neuroscientist | Perception, prediction, motor coordination | What information is available to the nervous system, and at what time scale? | Inferring subjective experience from physiology alone |
| Plant physiologist | Modular growth, abscission, autophagy, compartmentalization | What can be shed, recycled, isolated, or regrown? | Treating all plants or all shedding as equivalent |
| Comparative biologist | Shared ancestry, convergence, species differences | Is this homologous, analogous, or merely visually similar? | Assuming same appearance means same mechanism |
| Evolutionary cancer biologist | Somatic evolution and multicellular cooperation | Which level is being selected, and whose persistence is favored? | Reducing all cancer to one evolutionary story |
| Geroscientist | Aging mechanisms and functional decline | Which maintenance capacities change with age, and what evidence shows causality? | Treating aging as one pathway or one universal failure |
| Tissue engineer / regenerative biologist | Repair, replacement, patterning, integration | What must be rebuilt, connected, and functionally tested? | Equating new tissue or a changed biomarker with restored function |
| Measurement scientist | Latent variables, observability, validity | What can be inferred from available signals, and what remains unobservable? | Treating a proxy as the underlying state |
| Clinician / translational scientist | Human relevance and clinical endpoints | Does the model apply to patients, and does intervention improve outcomes? | Translating cell or animal findings directly into treatment advice |
| Statistician / causal-inference reviewer | Design, confounding, uncertainty | What alternative explanation fits the data? | Confusing association, selection, and causation |
| Historian of science / prior-art reviewer | Existing knowledge and conceptual lineage | Who has already studied this, under what names, and what is actually new? | Calling a reframing a discovery |
| Philosopher of biology / mind | Function, agency, experience, categories | Which statements are functional, normative, or experiential? | Smuggling consciousness or intention into biological descriptions |
| Ethicist / affected-community reviewer | Consequences, consent, harms, values | Who benefits, who bears risk, and what should not be inferred or exposed? | Treating technical feasibility as permission |
| Adversary | Falsification and counterexamples | What would make this interpretation fail? | Performing skepticism without a concrete disconfirmation test |
| Verifier | Independent evidence-to-claim audit | Does the cited evidence support the exact wording? | Treating a completed checklist as proof |
| Cartographer / recorder | Provenance and temporal continuity | Where did this path originate, how did it branch, and what changed? | Smoothing away contradiction or source uncertainty |

### Required role rotation

For each major claim, the process is:
1. Builder states the claim and intended scope.
2. Relevant domain minds generate mechanisms and alternatives.
3. Measurement and causal-inference minds define what can actually be observed.
4. Adversary searches for counterexamples and category errors.
5. Verifier checks source-to-claim fit.
6. Cartographer records the result, unresolved disagreement, and next discriminating test.

The same model may simulate several roles, but that is not independent review. Independence requires a genuinely separate reviewer, dataset, method, or replication.

## 3. Apply the minds to each conversation node

### N01 — Voice, breathing, body, and sound

- **Mechanistic question:** Which respiratory, laryngeal, articulatory, and postural actions generate the sound?
- **Control question:** Which signals regulate pressure, airflow, vocal-fold vibration, pitch, timing, and correction?
- **Neuroscience question:** How do motor commands, sensory feedback, prediction, and auditory monitoring interact?
- **Measurement question:** Which variables are directly measured—airflow, pressure, electromyography, acoustics, kinematics—and which are inferred?
- **Adversarial challenge:** Does vocal performance itself demonstrate improved health, or merely skilled task-specific coordination?
- **Research paths:** respiratory mechanics; voice production; sensorimotor integration; autonomic correlates of singing; longitudinal health outcomes in singers.
- **Boundary:** A physiological effect during singing does not establish a lifespan effect or generalized restoration.

### N02 — Open-loop versus closed-loop

- **Control theory:** A system can contain feedforward control, feedback correction, predictive control, and nested loops simultaneously.
- **Neuroscience:** Sensory feedback can be delayed, weighted, suppressed, or integrated with prediction.
- **Measurement science:** A loop is only demonstrated when the relevant input, controller, output, feedback path, and timing are specified.
- **Adversary:** “Open-loop” may be a false binary if the process is partly predictive and partly corrected.
- **Research path:** draw the actual control architecture for a bounded vocal task, then test perturbation response and recovery.
- **MC implication:** Store the loop diagram, time constants, observability limits, and perturbation evidence—not just a label.

### N03–N04 — Activation, singing, and restoration

- **Physiologist:** Separate acute activation, training adaptation, fatigue, recovery, and repair.
- **Geroscientist:** Ask whether repeated activity changes long-term function or merely produces transient state shifts.
- **Sleep researcher:** Determine which recovery processes are sleep-dependent and which are not.
- **Immunologist:** Examine inflammation and resolution, not inflammation alone.
- **Adversary:** If an activity feels energizing, what objective outcome would show durable benefit? What would show harm?
- **Research path:** define restoration as a vector of outcomes: return to baseline, improved capacity, repaired structure, reduced burden, preserved reserve, or improved task function. These are not interchangeable.
- **MC implication:** Never collapse “feels better,” “marker normalized,” “tissue repaired,” and “function restored” into one status.

### N05–N06 — Immune harm, clenching, persistence, and release

- **Immunologist:** Identify initiation signals, effector mechanisms, resolution pathways, regulatory cells, and tissue context.
- **Muscle physiologist:** Distinguish voluntary contraction, reflex activity, tone, fatigue, ischemia, and relaxation.
- **Autonomic neuroscientist:** Separate sympathetic activation, parasympathetic regulation, baroreflexes, and local tissue control.
- **Pain scientist:** Examine protective behavior, sensitization, and the possibility that a once-useful response becomes maladaptive.
- **Adversary:** “The body is never wrong” is not a testable rule. Biological processes can be locally functional yet globally harmful; they can also be dysregulated without being purposeless.
- **Research path:** investigate resolution as an active biological phase, not merely the absence of activation.
- **Boundary:** Muscular release, autonomic down-regulation, immune resolution, apoptosis, and autophagy are distinct processes. They may interact, but they are not interchangeable.

### N07–N10 — Comparative biology, plants, trees, leaves, hair, and continuity

- **Plant physiologist:** Study abscission zones, nutrient remobilization, autophagy, wound sealing, and compartmentalization.
- **Mammalian cell biologist:** Study apoptosis, epithelial extrusion, efferocytosis, autophagy, stem-cell replacement, and tissue remodeling.
- **Comparative biologist:** Classify each comparison as homologous mechanism, convergent functional analogy, or superficial resemblance.
- **Evolutionary biologist:** Ask how modularity, body plan, life history, and ecological niche alter the cost of local loss.
- **Philosopher of mind:** Separate physiological response from subjective grief or awareness.
- **Adversary:** Long-lived trees do not prove superior release, and visible shedding does not prove successful recovery.
- **Research path:** compare the full sequence: detection → boundary preparation → removal or isolation → sealing → replacement or compensation → functional assessment.
- **Existing evidence base:** Expedition 003 documents plant abscission, plant autophagy, tree compartmentalization, mammalian epithelial extrusion, and age-related immune clearance studies. These are distinct mechanisms connected by a provisional systems-level framework.
- **Boundary:** Plants and animals both regulate removal and maintenance; their mechanisms, architectures, and capacities differ.

### N11 — Cancer and the conflict between part and whole

- **Cancer biologist:** Examine somatic mutation, clonal selection, proliferation, apoptosis resistance, immune evasion, invasion, and treatment resistance.
- **Evolutionary theorist:** Ask how cell-level selection can undermine organism-level cooperation.
- **Immunologist:** Examine immune surveillance, immune editing, tolerance, and tumor microenvironment.
- **Tissue biologist:** Examine boundary integrity, extracellular matrix, cell competition, and tissue architecture.
- **Adversary:** Cancer is not one disease and cannot be reduced to “failure to release,” “unfinished healing,” or one immune defect.
- **Research path:** test which specific maintenance or cooperation failures are causal in defined tumor types and stages.
- **Boundary:** A conceptual model of multicellular cooperation does not by itself identify a treatment target.

### N12–N14 — Conversation as an evolving research object; MC and Proof Machine

- **Discourse analyst:** Preserve sequence, corrections, and shifts in question—not only extracted keywords.
- **Knowledge engineer:** Build nodes for observations, claims, hypotheses, sources, decisions, contradictions, and tests.
- **Human-AI interaction researcher:** Test whether the map improves retrieval, correction, and user control.
- **Adversary:** A coherent retrospective narrative can be manufactured after the fact. The original sequence and uncertainty must remain visible.
- **Verifier:** Check that each mapped move is traceable to an actual message or explicitly labeled summary.
- **Research path:** compare topic-only summaries with trajectory-aware maps on recovery accuracy, missed branches, correction latency, and unsupported inference.
- **MC implication:** The conversation itself becomes a provenance-bearing research object, not evidence that the biological hypotheses are true.

## 4. The research paths opened by the conversation

The map contains several connected programs. They should remain linked but not collapsed into one theory.

### Path A — Regulation and termination
**Question:** What determines whether a response starts, persists, changes intensity, and resolves?

Domains: control theory, autonomic physiology, motor control, immunology, inflammation resolution, pain science.

Potential measurable variables:
- initiating perturbation and its duration;
- response latency and amplitude;
- feedback availability and delay;
- persistence after stimulus removal;
- resolution half-time;
- return-to-baseline versus overshoot;
- recurrence after repeated perturbation;
- functional cost during and after response.

Discriminating test: compare matched systems with intact versus experimentally altered feedback or resolution mechanisms, using a defined perturbation and prespecified outcomes.

### Path B — Selective turnover and continuity
**Question:** How does a system remove or recycle components without losing tissue or organismal integrity?

Domains: autophagy, apoptosis, efferocytosis, extrusion, abscission, compartmentalization, stem-cell biology, tissue engineering.

Potential variables:
- target recognition specificity;
- removal rate;
- collateral damage;
- boundary closure;
- replacement rate;
- integration quality;
- function after turnover.

Discriminating test: alter one defined clearance or boundary mechanism and measure both removal success and whole-tissue function. Removal alone is not a sufficient endpoint.

### Path C — Recovery, regeneration, and reserve
**Question:** What is the difference between returning to baseline, repairing damage, replacing lost structures, and regaining function?

Domains: regenerative biology, stem-cell biology, wound healing, rehabilitation science, aging.

Potential variables:
- structural integrity;
- cell composition;
- tissue architecture;
- functional performance;
- reserve capacity under a second challenge;
- recurrence or delayed failure.

Discriminating test: use a second challenge after apparent recovery. A system that returns to baseline but loses reserve is not equivalent to one that restores capacity.

### Path D — Aging and clearance capacity
**Question:** Which maintenance and clearance processes decline with age, in which cell types and tissues, and with what causal consequences?

Domains: geroscience, macrophage biology, autophagy, efferocytosis, senescence, mitochondrial biology.

Current evidence: Expedition 003 includes two 2026 preclinical studies on age-related macrophage clearance pathways. They support specific mouse-model mechanisms, not a universal human aging theory or a proven human treatment.

Discriminating test: cell-type-specific intervention, appropriate controls, tissue-specific endpoints, functional outcomes, and independent replication; human evidence must be evaluated separately.

### Path E — Cancer as a multilevel conflict
**Question:** Under what conditions does cell-level persistence defeat tissue-level constraints and organism-level function?

Domains: somatic evolution, tumor ecology, immune surveillance, cell competition, tissue mechanics.

Potential variables:
- proliferation and death rates;
- clonal diversity and selection;
- boundary disruption;
- immune recognition and escape;
- invasion and metastasis;
- treatment response and resistance.

Discriminating test: defined cancer type, stage, mechanism, intervention, and outcome. Do not generalize across cancers.

### Path F — Invisible-state reconstruction
**Question:** How much can be inferred about a system's hidden state from incomplete, delayed, noisy, and context-dependent observations?

Domains: measurement theory, inverse problems, causal inference, Bayesian modeling, sensor fusion, systems identification.

Potential variables:
- observability;
- measurement noise;
- temporal sampling;
- proxy validity;
- missingness;
- model identifiability;
- uncertainty calibration.

Discriminating test: construct cases with known hidden states, hide or degrade selected observations, and evaluate whether the method correctly abstains or distinguishes competing states.

### Path G — Comparative architecture
**Question:** How do modularity, boundaries, and replacement strategies differ across plants, animals, tissues, and life histories?

Domains: comparative physiology, evolutionary developmental biology, plant science, zoology, ecology.

Discriminating test: matched functional questions across taxa, with species- and tissue-specific mechanisms recorded rather than presumed homologous.

### Path H — Human-AI co-investigation
**Question:** Does trajectory-aware, provenance-preserving conversation analysis improve research quality over ordinary summarization?

Domains: HCI, cognitive science, information retrieval, knowledge graphs, AI evaluation, research methods.

Possible outcomes:
- recovery of original questions and corrections;
- number of meaningful branches recovered;
- source-to-claim traceability;
- missed counterexamples;
- false connections;
- user correction burden;
- time to identify the next discriminating test.

Discriminating test: blinded comparison of topic summaries, chronological summaries, and provenance-linked trajectory maps on the same conversation corpus. Include human adjudication and privacy review.

## 5. Cross-path bridge: the common structure without claiming one mechanism

A recurring abstract sequence appears across several domains:

**signal or perturbation → state estimation → response selection → boundary/coordination → action → removal, repair, or containment → replacement/reorganization → functional reassessment → updated state**

This is a candidate comparative scaffold. It is not a universal biological pathway. Some systems lack one or more stages; stages may be simultaneous, distributed, or cyclic; and the same function may be implemented by different mechanisms.

### Three comparison labels

- **Mechanistic identity:** evidence supports the same or homologous mechanism.
- **Functional analogy:** different mechanisms contribute to a similar system-level function.
- **Conceptual analogy:** a useful way to ask a question, without evidence of shared function or mechanism.

Every cross-domain edge must carry one of these labels plus a confidence rationale. An unlabeled “connection” is not an accepted research result.

## 8. Proof Machine: tests that could make this map fail

### Test 1 — Transcript fidelity
- **Hypothesis:** The map preserves the actual order and meaning of the user's questions and corrections.
- **Attack:** Compare each node to the raw transcript; identify paraphrase drift, missing turns, reordered events, and assistant-introduced ideas incorrectly attributed to the user.
- **Pass:** Every node links to a source message or is explicitly labeled summary-derived.
- **Fail:** Any material user intent is invented, omitted without notice, or misattributed.

### Test 2 — Branch completeness
- **Hypothesis:** The map captures the materially distinct research paths opened by the conversation.
- **Attack:** Independent annotators create branches from the raw transcript without seeing this map.
- **Pass:** High overlap on major questions, with disagreements preserved.
- **Fail:** Important branches are absent or the map forces distinct questions into one narrative.

### Test 3 — Mechanism separation
- **Hypothesis:** The map does not confuse different processes under the word “release.”
- **Attack:** Ask domain reviewers to classify each named process and flag invalid equivalences.
- **Pass:** Every connection is labeled as mechanistic identity, functional analogy, or conceptual analogy.
- **Fail:** A metaphor is used as causal evidence.

### Test 4 — Evidence lineage
- **Hypothesis:** Every external factual claim can be traced to a source that supports its exact scope.
- **Attack:** Open each source; check study type, species, tissue, sample, intervention, comparator, endpoint, and limitations.
- **Pass:** Claims are narrowed where source scope is narrower.
- **Fail:** Review findings are presented as primary data, mouse findings as human efficacy, or correlations as causation.

### Test 5 — Counterexample pressure
- **Hypothesis:** Continuity through selective loss is a useful but bounded framework.
- **Attack:** Search for cases where removal is harmful, where regeneration occurs without major removal, where persistence is protective, or where tissue continuity fails despite successful clearance.
- **Pass:** Framework is narrowed to the contexts where it explains evidence.
- **Fail:** Framework is treated as a universal biological rule.

### Test 6 — Multi-perspective value
- **Hypothesis:** Explicit research roles improve error detection beyond a single unstructured synthesis.
- **Attack:** Compare single-pass synthesis with role-rotated review on the same evidence packet.
- **Pass:** Role-rotated review identifies additional material errors or improves calibration without excessive false positives.
- **Fail:** Personas only produce stylistic variety or reinforce the same assumptions.

### Test 7 — User control and privacy
- **Hypothesis:** The map can preserve meaningful private context without exposing raw sensitive details in public artifacts.
- **Attack:** Attempt to infer or reconstruct private identity-specific facts from the public-safe output.
- **Pass:** Public artifacts retain only necessary abstractions and provenance classes.
- **Fail:** Private transcript details, health data, household information, or identity-linked details leak into public research records.

### Test 8 — Actionability
- **Hypothesis:** The map yields discriminating next tests rather than an ever-expanding list of ideas.
- **Attack:** Ask each path to specify variables, comparator, outcome, confounders, feasibility, and disconfirmation.
- **Pass:** A bounded experiment or literature question can be executed.
- **Fail:** The path cannot identify what observation would change the conclusion.

## 9. Operating protocol for future turns in this research thread

For every new user turn:
1. Attach the turn to one or more existing nodes; create a new node only if it adds a genuinely new question.
2. Preserve the exact user correction and what it invalidates.
3. Record the assistant's prior claim separately from the user's claim.
4. Identify whether the turn changes the mechanism, scale, time window, population, or desired output.
5. Route it to the relevant research minds.
6. Search both supporting and disconfirming evidence where external claims are involved.
7. Update the claim register; do not silently overwrite a prior interpretation.
8. Generate the next discriminating question or test.
9. Keep symbolic meaning available in its own lane.
10. Record source status, claim status, privacy status, missingness, and revision reason.

### Required object types

- `ConversationNode`: source message, sequence, speaker, timestamp if available, exact/summary status.
- `Question`: explicit question and scope.
- `Correction`: what was challenged and what must change.
- `Concept`: stable term with definition and ambiguity notes.
- `Claim`: exact proposition, type, status, and scope.
- `EvidenceRecord`: source, method, sample, result, limitation, locator.
- `Hypothesis`: mechanism, alternatives, predictions, disconfirmation.
- `ResearchPath`: linked questions, disciplines, evidence gaps, next test.
- `PerspectiveReview`: role, critique, supporting basis, unresolved issue.
- `Contradiction`: two claims that cannot both hold under the same scope.
- `Decision`: what changed, why, and who authorized it.
- `SymbolicObject`: metaphor or image, with a strict non-evidence label.
- `Test`: design, comparator, outcomes, confounders, stopping rule, result.
- `RevisionEvent`: old state, new state, reason, evidence, date.

## 11. Current conclusions

1. The conversation has a coherent research trajectory: from coordinated biological output, through regulation and resolution, to selective turnover and continuity, then to cancer and the problem of preserving a whole while its parts change.
2. The strongest biological bridge is not a single “release” mechanism. It is the bounded systems question of how preservation, removal, containment, replacement, and functional reassessment are coordinated.
3. The strongest MC contribution at this stage is methodological: preserving the path from question to hypothesis to evidence to challenge to revision, without promoting metaphor or coherence into proof.
4. The most important next evidence task is a counterexample-rich comparative review, followed by transcript-fidelity and multi-perspective evaluation.
5. No universal biological law, human treatment, cancer cure, or validated MC capability is established by this artifact.

## 13. First counterexample audit — the timing of removal can reverse the outcome

**Audit date:** 2026-11-02  
**Purpose:** Stress-test the provisional continuity framework against evidence that removal, persistence, or clearance can be beneficial or harmful depending on context.

### Finding 1 — Some senescent cells actively support repair and regeneration

Da Silva-Álvarez et al. (2019; published in *Aging Cell*, issue date 2020), “Cell senescence contributes to tissue regeneration in zebrafish,” studied adult zebrafish fin amputation. Senescence-associated cells appeared transiently at the injury site; removing them with senolytic interventions impaired fin regeneration in the tested model. The authors discuss limitations of senescence markers and the possibility of off-target effects from senolytic compounds, so the result should not be generalized to all senescent cells or tissues. Primary paper: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6974711/ ; DOI https://doi.org/10.1111/acel.13052.

This is a direct counterexample to “remove senescent cells whenever detected.” A transient cell state can contribute to a repair program. The relevant variable is not simply presence versus absence, but cell identity, state, location, timing, and duration.

### Finding 2 — A well-known wound-healing paper now carries an expression of concern

The 2014 Demaria et al. paper, “An essential role for senescent cells in optimal wound healing through secretion of PDGF-AA” (Developmental Cell; PMID 25499914), is associated in PubMed with an **Expression of Concern** published 8 July 2026. PubMed lists the 2026 notice and explicitly links it as an expression of concern for the 2014 article: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42412492/.

**Proof Machine correction:** Do not treat the 2014 paper as clean, unqualified support. Any use of its findings must display the expression-of-concern status and the underlying concern must be inspected before drawing conclusions. The present audit has verified the notice exists, but has not yet retrieved and evaluated the publisher's full explanation. The evidence status is therefore flagged, not adjudicated.

This is an example of why provenance must be temporal: a source's status can change after it has entered a research dossier.

### Finding 3 — Senescence has context-dependent protective, reparative, and pathological roles

The review “Cellular Senescence in Aging, Tissue Repair, and Regeneration” summarizes evidence that senescence can suppress tumorigenesis, participate in development and repair, and become harmful when senescent cells persist. It reports the zebrafish regeneration result and notes unresolved questions about identifying truly senescent cells versus transient expression of markers. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9529244/.

A 2025 review likewise characterizes senescence as context-dependent, with transient senescence potentially supporting regeneration and chronic senescence contributing to tissue deterioration: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40318767/.

These are reviews, not new independent experiments. They help map the state of the field but do not multiply the number of independent primary replications.

### Finding 4 — Efferocytosis can be redirected in tumors

Yang et al. (2026), “Metabolic reprogramming of efferocytosis in the tumour microenvironment,” is a review describing how tumors can exploit apoptotic-cell clearance and associated metabolic signaling to promote immunosuppressive macrophage states, tumor progression, and treatment resistance. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12848541/ ; DOI https://doi.org/10.1002/ctm2.70601.

This supports a bounded conclusion: a normal homeostatic process can be co-opted by a pathological environment. It does **not** establish that efferocytosis is generally harmful, nor that all tumors exploit it in the same way. The next step is to extract and audit the primary studies underlying each specific mechanism rather than treating the review's synthesis as independent proof.

### Finding 5 — Plant abscission is not automatically beneficial

Li & Su (2024) describe abscission as a regulated plant process that can support nutrient allocation and reproduction, while also noting that excessive or poorly timed abscission can reduce crop quality and yield. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11740850/ ; DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s44307-024-00033-9.

This prevents the plant analogy from becoming “shedding is healthy.” The consequence depends on what is shed, when, under what conditions, and what the remaining plant can sustain.

### Revised synthesis after counterexample pressure

The original proposition—continuity depends in part on coordinating preservation, selective removal, containment, replacement, and reintegration—survives only with a stronger qualification:

**Continuity depends on context-appropriate control of component state and fate. Removal is one possible outcome, not the default objective. Persistence may be protective, reparative, neutral, or pathological; clearance may be restorative, mistimed, incomplete, or co-opted.**

The framework therefore needs at least these state variables:
- identity and functional state of the component;
- tissue and organismal context;
- timing relative to injury, development, infection, or tumor evolution;
- duration and reversibility of the state;
- clearance or persistence mechanism;
- neighboring-cell and immune response;
- boundary integrity;
- replacement and integration capacity;
- whole-system function after the event.

### New adversarial tests

1. **False-positive removal:** What happens when a useful transient cell state is classified as harmful and eliminated?
2. **Clearance hijack:** Can a tumor or pathogen redirect a normal clearance pathway toward its own advantage?
3. **Persistence benefit:** Which states must persist long enough to perform repair, patterning, or tumor suppression?
4. **Timing inversion:** Does the same intervention help at one phase and harm at another?
5. **Source-status drift:** Has a cited paper acquired a correction, expression of concern, retraction, or updated interpretation since the dossier was written?
6. **Marker ambiguity:** Are markers sufficient to identify the biological state, or can they be transiently expressed by other cell types?

### Claim register amendments

- **C004-09 — “More clearance is always better”**: Rejected. Counterexamples show that removal of transient reparative senescent cells can impair regeneration in a zebrafish model, and normal efferocytosis can be redirected in tumor contexts.
- **C004-10 — “A detected senescence marker identifies a harmful senescent cell”**: Rejected. Marker expression and functional cell state must be distinguished; cell identity, location, timing, and function matter.
- **C004-11 — “The 2014 Demaria wound-healing study is unqualified supporting evidence”**: Blocked pending review of the 2026 expression-of-concern notice.
- **C004-12 — “Abscission is intrinsically adaptive”**: Rejected as universal wording. Plant abscission can be useful or yield-reducing depending on context.

- **Primary study:** Da Silva-Álvarez et al. 2019/2020 zebrafish regeneration study. Supports a model-specific claim.
- **Review:** López? No—do not merge this review with a primary result. The 2022 review is a synthesis and should be counted as secondary evidence.
- **Primary study with current integrity flag:** Demaria et al. 2014 wound-healing paper; expression of concern indexed in 2026. Do not rely on it without inspecting the notice.
- **Review:** Yang et al. 2026 tumor efferocytosis review; use it to locate primary mechanisms, not as proof of every mechanism it summarizes.
