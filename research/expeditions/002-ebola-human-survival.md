# Expedition 002 — Ebola Human Survival: Viral Control, Host Protection, Recovery

**Project:** Mirror Cartographer  
**Status:** Initial evidence-grounded research dossier; not a systematic review  
**Created:** 2026-11-02  
**Research question:** Which combinations of early viral control, physiological support, and post-acute follow-up most improve survival and recovery in human orthoebolavirus disease—and what evidence is still missing?

> **Scope and safety boundary:** This dossier synthesizes public clinical, epidemiological, and mechanistic evidence. It is not a treatment protocol, a clinical recommendation, or a claim that Mirror Cartographer has discovered a cure. It does not provide pathogen-handling or experimental infection instructions. Clinical decisions and research involving filoviruses belong to qualified teams, authorized facilities, national health authorities, and ethics-approved trials.

## 1. The first correction: “Ebola” is not one interchangeable treatment target

The evidence base is species-specific.

- **Orthoebolavirus zairense (Zaire ebolavirus):** two monoclonal-antibody products, Inmazeb (REGN-EB3) and Ebanga (ansuvimab / mAb114), are FDA-approved for disease caused by this species. Their efficacy cannot simply be transferred to other orthoebolaviruses.
- **Orthoebolavirus bundibugyoense (Bundibugyo virus):** the major 2026 outbreak is caused by this species. As of 2026-11-02, no specifically approved treatment or vaccine exists. WHO has prioritized investigational candidates for clinical evaluation.
- **Orthoebolavirus sudanense (Sudan virus)** and **Orthoebolavirus taiense (Taï Forest virus):** human disease is documented, but there are no FDA-approved therapeutics or vaccines for these species.

The current Bundibugyo outbreak makes this an immediate translational research problem, not merely a hypothetical comparison. ECDC's 2026-10-29 update reported 10,193 confirmed cases and 4,960 related deaths in the DRC using data through 2026-10-27; the agency noted that figures were under continuous review and harmonisation. These are outbreak surveillance counts, not a controlled estimate of treatment efficacy.

## 2. What is already demonstrated in humans

### 2.1 Zaire ebolavirus: monoclonal antibodies improve survival

**PALM randomized controlled trial (Mulangu et al., NEJM, 2019)**

Source: https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1910993  
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31774950/  
Trial: NCT03719586

In the PALM trial, 28-day mortality was:

| Trial group | Deaths / participants | Mortality |
|---|---:|---:|
| mAb114 / ansuvimab | 61 / 174 | 35.1% |
| ZMapp comparator | 84 / 169 | 49.7% |
| REGN-EB3 | 52 / 155 | 33.5% |
| Concurrent ZMapp subgroup comparator | 79 / 154 | 51.3% |
| Remdesivir | 93 / 175 | 53.1% |

**Interpretation:** mAb114 and REGN-EB3 reduced mortality relative to the relevant ZMapp comparator groups. Remdesivir did not show a survival advantage over ZMapp in this Zaire-virus trial.

Baseline viral load, duration of symptoms before admission, serum creatinine, and aminotransferase levels were associated with outcome. High viral load was a powerful marker of poor prognosis, but it did not mean antibody treatment was futile: mortality in the high-viral-load subgroup was still lower with mAb114 (69.9%) and REGN-EB3 (63.6%) than in the corresponding ZMapp comparator groups (84.5% and 86.2%).

**Important correction to a tempting interpretation:** these subgroup data do not prove a biological point-of-no-return threshold, nor do they show that antibodies stop working at high viral burden. They show that high baseline viral burden marks severe disease and that treatment benefit can coexist with a much worse underlying prognosis. A threshold model remains a hypothesis to test, not a finding.

### 2.2 Bundibugyo virus: active clinical evaluation, not established efficacy

WHO's May 2026 technical advisory group prioritized these candidates for clinical-trial evaluation:

- **MBP134:** an investigational broadly neutralizing monoclonal-antibody cocktail.
- **Maftivimab:** a monoclonal antibody that is one component of the Zaire-approved REGN-EB3 combination; cross-species activity is a research rationale, not proof of clinical efficacy against Bundibugyo virus.
- **Remdesivir:** an antiviral candidate supported by preclinical evidence and prior clinical experience in other contexts.
- **Combination evaluation:** WHO specifically recommended evaluating a monoclonal antibody plus remdesivir.
- **Obeldesivir:** prioritized for evaluation as post-exposure prophylaxis among contacts, not as a proven treatment for established disease.

WHO's PARTNERS adaptive randomized trial began enrolling patients in the DRC on 2026-07-02 to evaluate MBP134, remdesivir, and their combination. Public reporting found 250 participants enrolled by 2026-08-28. No efficacy result was identified in the sources reviewed for this dossier as of 2026-11-02.

Sources:
- WHO candidate prioritization: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/B09767
- WHO trial launch: https://www.who.int/news/item/02-07-2026-patient-enrolment-begins-in-a-scientific-trial-to-identify-the-first-effective-treatments-for-bundibugyo-virus-disease
- WHO candidate recommendations: https://www.who.int/news/item/28-05-2026-experts-convened-by-who-advise-on-candidate-treatments-and-vaccines-for-ebola-disease-caused-by-bundibugyo-virus
- ECDC outbreak update: https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/en/public-health-events/2026-eip-00026/2026-10-29

**Interpretation:** the strongest current opportunity is to learn from the ongoing Bundibugyo trial and its clinical data, not to invent a speculative drug combination in parallel without access to those results.

## 3. Human disease map: linked processes, not a single linear cascade

A useful clinical model separates viral activity from host injury and recovery. The processes interact, but they are not interchangeable.

| Layer | What is observed or established | What remains uncertain |
|---|---|---|
| Viral entry and spread | Viral glycoprotein mediates entry; infected immune-lineage cells contribute to early dissemination | Which early human measurements best identify the transition to systemic disease |
| Viral replication | Viral RNA replication depends on viral proteins and host-cell machinery | Which additional antiviral strategies improve human outcomes beyond antibody treatment |
| Innate immune antagonism | EBOV VP35 interferes with interferon induction; VP24 interferes with STAT1 nuclear signaling | Whether safely reversing these mechanisms is therapeutically useful in humans |
| Systemic inflammation and immune dysregulation | Severe disease involves complex inflammatory and immune changes | Which immune signals are causal drivers, compensatory responses, or markers of burden |
| Fluid, vascular and circulatory dysfunction | Vomiting/diarrhoea, hypovolaemia, vascular fluid shifts, shock and electrolyte abnormalities can be life-threatening | Which early measurements best anticipate deterioration and which host-directed interventions add benefit |
| Organ injury | Kidney and liver injury markers are associated with poor outcomes | How much injury is reversible after viral control, and when |
| Recovery and persistence | Survivors can have prolonged symptoms; viral material and, in some specimens, infectious virus have been detected after acute disease | Which survivors are at risk, how to distinguish residual RNA from viable virus, and how to treat persistent infection safely |

Mechanistic source for VP35 / VP24: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41572-020-0147-3

Clinical guidance source: CDC, 2026-05-27: https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html

## 4. The clinical treatment architecture

### Layer A — Reduce infectious virus

For Zaire ebolavirus, use of the approved neutralizing monoclonal antibodies is supported by randomized human trial evidence and regulatory approval.

For Bundibugyo virus, MBP134, maftivimab, remdesivir, and selected combinations are investigational candidates under clinical evaluation. They must not be described as proven treatments.

The distinction between neutralizing extracellular virus and inhibiting intracellular replication is useful as a conceptual treatment map. It does not establish that combining both will necessarily improve survival; that is precisely what the adaptive clinical trial is designed to test.

### Layer B — Preserve physiological function while viral control occurs

The WHO 2026 filovirus clinical-management guideline emphasizes systematic clinical and laboratory monitoring, individualized oral and intravenous fluid therapy, management of shock, vasopressors where indicated, treatment of suspected bacterial co-infection, management of haemorrhagic complications, and survivor follow-up.

CDC clinical guidance also identifies hypovolaemia, electrolyte abnormalities, hematologic abnormalities, refractory shock, hypoxia, haemorrhage, septic shock, multiorgan failure, and disseminated intravascular coagulation among complications requiring clinical management.

This layer is not secondary comfort care. It is part of life-saving disease management.

Guidelines:
- WHO 2026 guideline: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/B09774
- CDC clinical guidance: https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html

### Layer C — Restore function and manage post-acute disease

CDC's 2026-09-11 survivor guidance documents persistence in immunologically privileged sites, including the eye, central nervous system, testes, placenta and other compartments. The document distinguishes detection of viral RNA or antigen from isolation of infectious virus. Those are not equivalent endpoints.

Examples in CDC's evidence table include:
- Ebola RNA detected in cerebrospinal fluid up to 10 months after illness onset; no published infectious-virus isolation in that specimen category in the table.
- Viral RNA detected in seminal fluid up to 40 months; infectious virus isolated up to 82 days after illness onset in the cited data.
- Infectious virus isolated from aqueous humour at 14 weeks after illness onset in a reported case.

These observations justify structured survivor follow-up and research into persistent infection. They do not justify assuming that every survivor has infectious virus or that a positive PCR necessarily means transmissible virus.

Source: https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/hcp/clinical-guidance/management-of-survivors.html

## 5. The research opportunity: model the gap between viral control and host recovery

### Central question

**When viral burden falls, which patients continue to deteriorate, which recover, and what measurable processes explain the difference?**

This is a better-supported question than assuming a single irreversible threshold. It can be studied using longitudinal clinical data without inventing a new treatment first.

### Provisional hypotheses

**H1 — Trajectory adds information beyond a baseline snapshot.**  
Repeated measures of viral burden, renal function, liver injury, fluid/electrolyte status, coagulation, and clinical physiology may predict outcome better than any single admission measurement.

- Alternative: baseline severity and access-to-care variables explain most of the predictive value.
- Disconfirmation: longitudinal features do not improve externally validated prediction over a prespecified baseline model.

**H2 — Viral clearance and organ recovery are partially separable processes.**  
Some patients may show improving viral measures while kidney, liver, vascular, or circulatory dysfunction continues. The degree and duration of this divergence may relate to outcome.

- Alternative: apparent divergence is caused by measurement timing, assay limitations, or confounding by baseline severity.
- Disconfirmation: after accounting for timing and severity, viral and organ trajectories remain tightly coupled and add no distinct prognostic information.

**H3 — Earlier access to competent care is a modifiable determinant of outcome.**  
Time from symptom onset to diagnosis, referral, and optimized supportive care may interact with viral burden and organ injury.

- Alternative: apparent delay effects reflect geography, outbreak setting, case ascertainment, or severity-related selection.
- Disconfirmation: carefully adjusted analyses show no robust relationship between care timing and outcome.

**H4 — Post-acute persistence is a distinct clinical state, not simply slow convalescence.**  
A subset of survivors may have compartment-specific persistence or relapse risk that requires separate follow-up from general post-Ebola sequelae.

- Alternative: symptoms are predominantly post-infectious injury or other conditions rather than ongoing viral replication.
- Disconfirmation: longitudinal clinical assessment and validated testing fail to link suspected persistence to active viral replication.

## 6. A safe, reviewable analysis plan

This plan uses existing de-identified, ethically governed clinical and trial data. It does not require handling live virus or conducting new pathogen experiments.

1. **Define the population and species.** Keep Zaire and Bundibugyo cohorts separate. Do not pool species by default.
2. **Build a provenance-preserving variable dictionary.** For every field, record definition, unit, assay, collection time, missingness, and source.
3. **Separate baseline predictors from time-varying measurements.** Record symptom-onset estimate, first clinical contact, admission, treatment start, and each subsequent measurement with uncertainty in dates.
4. **Define outcomes before analysis.** At minimum: 28-day survival, time to clinical deterioration, organ-support requirements where recorded, discharge status, and post-discharge complications.
5. **Model trajectories, not just correlations.** Compare baseline-only models with longitudinal models; account for time-dependent treatment, informative missingness, site effects, and changes in standard of care.
6. **Validate externally.** Use held-out sites or time periods; avoid leakage from repeated measurements of the same patient across train/test partitions.
7. **Test transportability.** Evaluate separately by viral species, age group, pregnancy status where ethically and statistically appropriate, care setting, and outbreak period.
8. **Report uncertainty and failure.** Publish calibration, discrimination, missing-data sensitivity, subgroup limitations, and negative results. A prediction model is not a treatment effect.
9. **Use trial results to update treatment hypotheses.** Do not infer efficacy from compassionate use, animal models, in-vitro neutralization, or a trial's recruitment speed.
10. **Obtain independent clinical and statistical review.** Any prospective intervention must be developed and evaluated under appropriate regulatory, ethics, biosafety, and community-governance frameworks.

## 7. The evidence gaps ranked by importance

| Priority | Gap | Why it matters | Best next evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | Human efficacy of candidate treatment for Bundibugyo | No approved species-specific therapeutic; current outbreak | PARTNERS trial results and independent analysis |
| P0 | How early care, viral burden, and organ injury jointly relate to survival | High mortality and strong baseline prognostic associations | Patient-level longitudinal cohort and trial data |
| P1 | Incremental benefit of antibody + polymerase inhibitor | Mechanistically plausible but not established | Randomized comparison in the adaptive trial |
| P1 | Which supportive-care processes most change outcome in resource-constrained settings | Treatment efficacy depends on care delivery | WHO guideline evidence, implementation studies, site-level outcomes |
| P1 | Which post-acute symptoms reflect active persistence versus immune/tissue sequelae | Different causes require different management | Longitudinal survivor cohorts with appropriately interpreted testing |
| P2 | Safe host-directed immunomodulation | Immune suppression and harmful inflammation may coexist | Human observational evidence followed by carefully justified trials |
| P2 | VP35 / VP24-directed therapeutics | Strong mechanistic rationale, no established human treatment | Drug-discovery and translational evidence reviewed by specialists |

## 8. What would count as a genuine breakthrough?

Not a more elaborate pathway diagram. Not a plausible molecular target. Not an AI-generated hypothesis.

A meaningful result would be one of these:

- A reproducible, externally validated early-warning model that identifies deterioration soon enough to change care.
- A randomized clinical result showing that a candidate treatment or combination improves survival or another patient-important outcome.
- Evidence that a defined supportive-care intervention reduces preventable organ failure or death.
- A validated way to distinguish active persistent infection from residual viral RNA and post-infectious injury, leading to safer survivor care.
- A treatment for a currently untreated human orthoebolavirus species with demonstrated clinical benefit.

## 9. Current conclusion

The highest-value immediate work is to connect three evidence streams that are often treated separately:

1. **Antiviral efficacy:** what actually reduces infectious viral disease in a given species?
2. **Host-system trajectory:** what happens to kidney, liver, circulation, coagulation, and immune function over time?
3. **Care delivery and recovery:** how do timing, access, supportive care, and post-acute follow-up change outcomes?

The most important current opportunity is the ongoing Bundibugyo treatment trial, paired with careful longitudinal analysis of human outcomes. The most important caution is that the Zaire-virus antibody evidence cannot be assumed to apply to Bundibugyo, Sudan, or Taï Forest viruses.

**Status:** Research question defined; current clinical evidence and candidate pipeline mapped; testable hypotheses and a de-identified-data analysis plan specified. No new treatment or cure established.

It does not include a formal risk-of-bias appraisal of all studies, a complete search strategy, all null or contradictory studies, patient-level trial data, or independent expert review.
