# The Observatory of Almost-Known Things
*A field guide to Mirror Cartographer*

> What if the information survived, but the understanding did not?

This repository is the **workbench beneath the world**: the place where a feeling becomes a question, a question meets evidence, a pattern earns or loses confidence, and a useful idea becomes something another person can inspect.

It is not a shrine to certainty. It is an instrument for seeing what a single record cannot show by itself.

## A reading path for a curious mind

1. Start with the question: **What is visible here—and what would have to be true for my interpretation to hold?**
2. Choose one object: a claim, a fragment, a mechanism, a failure, a symbol.
3. Follow its source before following its story.
4. Look for the missing variable, the competing explanation, and the condition that would change your mind.
5. Leave a trace: what changed, what did not, and what should happen next.

You do not need to understand the entire repository before entering it. Pick a thread; let the thread reveal the room.

## The working vocabulary

- **Signal** — something observed.
- **Trace** — the record that lets another person inspect the signal.
- **Claim** — a statement that can be examined.
- **Hypothesis** — a possible explanation still waiting for a fair test.
- **Gap** — information whose absence changes what can responsibly be concluded.
- **Bridge** — a proposed relationship between domains, with its assumptions visible.
- **Counterlight** — evidence or a perspective that makes the first interpretation look different.
- **Nullglow** — the faint shape left by something expected but not found.
- **Glyphfracture** — the moment a symbol breaks open into more than one reading.
- **Driftroot** — a recurring pattern whose origin is not yet known.
- **Tonefold** — when a change in wording changes the meaning a person can reach.
- **Lurecraft** — the art of making a question inviting without making its answer predetermined.

These coined terms are working instruments, not scientific terminology. Use them to notice; translate them into plain language when precision matters.

## The rules that keep the work honest

- Observation is not interpretation. Interpretation is not proof.
- Similarity is not causation.
- Uncertainty belongs in the result, not hidden in the footnotes.
- A missing record is not evidence that an event did not happen.
- A beautiful connection still has to survive contact with sources.
- Sensitive personal, medical, veterinary, financial, or conversational material does not belong in public commits.
- A failed tool or dead end is not wasted if it reveals a requirement.

## The sentence at the center

**Language must mirror, not trap. Truth survives when it rotates.**

Mirror Cartographer began as a symbolic reflection interface and grew into a continuity system for fragmented information. Its work spans biomedical questions, public-interest systems, human–technology interaction, evidence design, and creative inquiry. The common problem is not a lack of data alone. It is the loss of context between one piece and the next.

This repository holds the method and the machinery. Its companion repository holds more of the encounter: rooms, experiments in language, and ways to feel a question before trying to solve it.
