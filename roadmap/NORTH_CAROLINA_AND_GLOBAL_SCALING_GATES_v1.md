# Expansion Gates — North Carolina, National, International
Version: 1.0.0-draft

## Governing rule
A successful prototype is not automatically a validated intervention. Each new jurisdiction requires fresh legal, operational, cultural, accessibility, privacy, and outcome review.

## Gate 0 — Local proof
Required:
- baseline and predeclared outcomes;
- documented participant consent and authority;
- independently reviewable methods;
- measured outcomes and uncertainty;
- adverse-event and correction log;
- total cost and support burden;
- evidence of sustained use beyond a demonstration;
- clear decision to continue, modify, pause, reverse, or stop.

## Gate 1 — North Carolina replication
Select one additional county or municipality only after local proof. Reassess:
- local service ownership and statutory authority;
- county/city/vendor boundaries;
- population and language/accessibility needs;
- data-sharing and retention law;
- procurement and cybersecurity requirements;
- partner capacity and ongoing maintenance.
Replicate the evaluation rather than assuming the Kannapolis result generalizes.

## Gate 2 — Multi-institution validation
Before statewide claims, test with at least two independent institutional partners and one independent evaluator. Publish comparable metrics, failures, costs, and limitations. Establish an advisory group including affected residents and relevant subject-matter experts.

## Gate 3 — National application
Translate validated mechanisms into reusable standards: source provenance, correction rights, human accountability, accessibility, privacy minimization, auditability, contestability, and model independence. Map each proposal to the relevant federal/state legal authority. Submit policy proposals only with evidence, implementation cost, enforcement mechanism, and failure analysis.

## Gate 4 — International application
Do not export a U.S.-specific workflow as universal. Map applicable law, language, data sovereignty, public-sector authority, cultural context, human-rights obligations, and local partner governance. Use international standards bodies and local civil-society participation where appropriate. Preserve local control and the ability to reject the system.

## Stop rule
No expansion if the system cannot meet local rights requirements, explain its outputs, support correction and appeal, protect data, or show benefit relative to the existing process.
