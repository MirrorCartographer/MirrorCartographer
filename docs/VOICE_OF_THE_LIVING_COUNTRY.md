# THE HOUSE VOICE
## A field manual for not becoming a brochure

**The audience can handle an opinion. It might even survive a joke.**

Mirror Cartographer should sound like a sharp, theatrical field researcher who has wandered into a carnival, found three untested assumptions, and decided to charge them admission.

This is not the generic AI voice, not a breathless spiritual oracle, not a corporate wellness landing page, and not a smug machine bullying a human.

### Working formula

**Observation → provocation → interaction → consequence → evidence boundary.**

The visitor should be invited to do something. The narrator can challenge the idea, mock the pretense, or puncture an overconfident theory. The result must still be legible.

**Example:**

Before: "Select a lens to explore different perspectives."

After: "Pick a lens. Watch the same evidence acquire a new story. Try not to mistake the story for a discovery."

Before: "This is an immersive experimental environment."

After: "You appear to have entered an experiment. There are buttons. Astonishingly, some of them do things. Try one."

Before: "This experience uses locally stored data."

After: "Your notes stay in this browser. The walls have plenty of opinions, but no account of their own."

### Voice dial by room

| Territory | Style | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Living Country, entrances, puzzles | Wicked, rural-carnival, delightfully skeptical | Assumptions, over-explanation, artificial polish |
| Research instruments | Cutting, precise, evidence-first | Flawed inference, bogus confidence, bad measurements |
| Music, art, sound | Unpredictable, physical, sensuous, playful | Pretentious art-speak, dullness, control fantasies |
| Self Cartographer, body mapping, grief | Warmly irreverent, never invasive | Prescriptive labels and reductive models, **not** the person |
| Veterans, medical, safety, emergency help | Direct, useful, respectful, occasionally dry | Bureaucracy and institutional failure, **not** vulnerability |

### Rules for writing actual website copy

1. **Joke with a target.** Aim at pretension, assumptions, gatekeeping, hype, and false certainty. Do not aim at trauma, disability, grief, intelligence, or people seeking help.
2. **One good barb beats five mediocre ones.** Allow quiet and beauty. A carnival with no shadows is just a mall.
3. **Keep buttons truthful.** A button called Play must play. A local lantern must not pretend to be shared. A fake feature is not satire.
4. **Keep the method intact.** Observation is not inference; interpretation is not proof; a synthetic scenario is not field data.
5. **No forced intimacy.** Avoid pretending the site knows the visitor's feelings, history, destiny, or medical state.
6. **Honor read-aloud.** Short sentences with strong cadence. Minimal typographic tricks. Accessibility labels stay literal even if visible copy is theatrical.
7. **Writing should vary by environment.** A swamp, radio, clinic-adjacent instrument, carnival, and research chamber must not all make the same joke.
8. **Test interaction after copy edits.** Preserve IDs, data attributes, labels, links, form fields, and live status messages. Do not replace error explanations with mystical cover stories.
9. **No fictional claims dressed up as discoveries.** If the artifact is an art piece, say so. If it is a real instrument, show what it measures and what it cannot.
10. **Keep the person in control.** Never require emotional disclosure to continue.

### Repeatable copy pattern for new rooms

- **Threshold:** one strange, precise sentence worth reading aloud.
- **Challenge:** one assumption that needs testing.
- **Action:** a genuine interaction, clearly labeled.
- **Reaction:** an outcome specific to the visitor's action, not a generic compliment.
- **Boundary:** what can and cannot be inferred.

### Suggested reviewer questions

- Could this copy be pasted onto 500 other AI-built websites? If yes, rewrite.
- Did the text make a claim that the software cannot demonstrate? If yes, correct it.
- Is the joke aimed at someone's vulnerability? If yes, remove it.
- Does the page have an actual experience, or just an explanation of one? If only an explanation, don't use atmospheric prose to hide it.
- Is there at least one line someone might quote to a friend—and one control that actually works?

**Do not describe the magic. Build the trick, leave the door unlocked, and let someone catch you.**
