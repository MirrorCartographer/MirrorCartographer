# Self-cartography: how a person understands and interacts with themselves

**Status:** working model and design contract  
**Companion experience:** `mirror-cartographer-ui/public/self/index.html`

## The central proposition

A person is not a static profile waiting to be classified. A person is a changing system in relationship with a changing world.

Self-understanding is the construction and revision of a useful model from partial observations across time. Self-interaction is the deliberate act of participating in that system: noticing conditions, changing a response or environment, observing consequences, and revising the model without confusing the model for the person.

The map must remain smaller than the territory.

## Seven dimensions of observation

The first public instrument exposes six entry lenses and keeps time as the dimension that makes patterns visible:

1. **Body:** energy, sensation, sleep, appetite, tension, pain, movement, recovery and other embodied signals.
2. **Attention:** attraction, avoidance, curiosity, focus, overload, salience and return.
3. **Environment:** light, sound, temperature, space, tools, pace, demands, access and friction.
4. **Relationships:** connection, distance, trust, roles, conflict, belonging, care and mutual influence.
5. **Values:** priorities, boundaries, trade-offs, fairness, freedom, beauty, responsibility and play.
6. **Action:** habits, decisions, workarounds, delays, experiments, rituals and repair.
7. **Time:** sequence, recurrence, duration, anticipation, lag, transition, seasonality and change.

These dimensions overlap. A bodily state may alter attention; attention may change action; action may change a relationship; a relationship may change the environment. The model should make these links inspectable without asserting a single cause.

## The observation grammar

Every trace should preserve four separable layers:

- **Scene:** what happened, where, when, and under what conditions.
- **Signal:** what was directly noticed or measured.
- **Interpretation:** what the person currently thinks it may mean.
- **Next contact:** what observation, comparison, low-risk variation, or conversation could refine the interpretation.

A metaphor can help someone hold an experience. It is not evidence for a mechanism. A measured signal can be useful. It is not the whole experience.

## How a person interacts with themselves

The cycle is:

`notice → externalize → contextualize → compare → vary → observe consequence → revise`

- **Notice** without requiring an explanation.
- **Externalize** by naming or drawing a temporary trace.
- **Contextualize** by recording the conditions around it.
- **Compare** recurrence and difference across situations and time.
- **Vary** one low-risk condition when possible; never turn self-observation into coercion.
- **Observe consequence** without assuming the experiment succeeded.
- **Revise** the map, including deleting or renaming a trace.

The observer is also part of the system. Attention changes what is noticed; recording can change what is remembered; an experiment can change the conditions being studied. The tool must disclose these effects rather than pretend to be neutral.

## Product behavior

The instrument should:
- allow entry through an image, event, body signal, task, relationship, value, or curiosity—not force one universal questionnaire;
- let the reader discover questions from juxtaposition instead of repeatedly asking them;
- keep notes optional and locally stored by default;
- provide export and clear deletion;
- never turn traces into diagnoses, personality scores, or deterministic predictions;
- show uncertainty and competing interpretations;
- support text, audio, visual, tactile and movement-based forms as the project grows;
- preserve a route back to ordinary life and never imply that continued use is necessary.

## Audience translation

The underlying model remains consistent while the entry vocabulary changes:

- **Engineer / coder:** state, feedback, observability, dependencies, latency, invariants and failure modes.
- **Military / operations:** situation, constraints, tempo, readiness, mission, handoff, after-action review and recovery.
- **Artist / musician:** motif, medium, rhythm, contrast, material, improvisation and revision.
- **Teacher:** learning conditions, attention, scaffolding, practice, feedback and transfer.
- **Hairstylist / craft worker:** material behavior, consultation, technique, transformation, maintenance and the relationship between intention and result.

These are not stereotypes or personality categories. They are alternative doors into the same system. A person can move between them.

## Evaluation

A self-cartography feature is not successful because it feels profound or produces a beautiful map. Test whether a person can:

1. create a trace without being forced to explain it;
2. distinguish observation from interpretation;
3. connect two traces while keeping uncertainty visible;
4. revise or remove a trace;
5. export their work and understand where it is stored;
6. identify one useful next observation or action;
7. leave without penalty, pressure, or loss of agency.

Measure usability, comprehension, accessibility, retention by voluntary return, and reported usefulness. Do not optimize for compulsion, dependency, distress, or prolonged engagement at the expense of agency.

## Open research questions

- Which representations help people notice cross-domain patterns without encouraging false causal stories?
- How does modality—text, image, sound, movement, spatial arrangement—change recall and comprehension?
- What kinds of feedback support agency rather than compliance?
- How can a system preserve a person's changing self-model without freezing an old version of them?
- Which patterns generalize across people, and which must remain individually contextual?

The enduring rule: **the person is not the data structure. The data structure exists to help the person see, test, and change what they choose.**
