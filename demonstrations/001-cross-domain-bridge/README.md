# Demonstration 001: The hidden interface

**Type:** synthetic method demonstration, not an empirical finding.

## Question

Can a system reveal a cross-domain interface that is easy to miss when each domain is inspected separately?

## Synthetic case

A fictional service pathway links a healthcare observation to a care plan, an administrative handoff, an eligibility review, service access, and a later follow-up. The graph engine calculates betweenness centrality on observed and supported edges only. A speculative direct link from the initial observation to service access is deliberately excluded.

## What becomes visible

The pathway's administrative handoff and eligibility review are candidate structural bridges. That does **not** prove either one causes delay, failure, or harm. It identifies where a researcher should inspect records, process definitions, waiting times, and counterexamples.

The interesting shift is from asking only “Which domain is failing?” to asking “Which translation or handoff between domains may be structurally consequential?” This is a research question generated by graph structure, not a conclusion about real services.

## Run

```bash
node --test tools/relationship-engine/relationship-engine.test.mjs

```

## Falsification and rival explanations

- The apparent bridge may be an artifact of missing nodes or incomplete sampling.
- A different edge definition may change the ranking.
- A direct route or parallel process may make the handoff nonessential.
- The handoff may be structurally central but operationally fast and harmless.
- Any real-world claim requires source-linked case records, defined populations and time windows, outcome measures, and comparison against alternative pathways.
