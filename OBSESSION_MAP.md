# The Obsession Map
*A trail through the questions that refused to stay separate*

This is not a backlog. It is a map of recurring gravitational pulls in the work: the ideas that keep reappearing in different forms because one framing has never been enough.

Read it like a constellation. You can enter anywhere. The point is to notice what each star makes visible in the others.


For a grounded inventory of what already exists, open the [Cabinet of Real Things](CABINET_OF_REAL_THINGS.md).

## First orbit — the impossible question

**What can be understood from individual facts, and how much remains invisible?**

A symptom is not the body. A record is not the event. A benefit policy is not the life of the person trying to reach it. A dataset is not the world it describes.

The project keeps returning to the space between the thing and the record of the thing. That space is not empty: it contains context, timing, missingness, competing causes, power, language, and change.

Begin with the [research method](research/METHOD.md). Then visit the [claim register](research/claim-register.json) and ask what each claim can actually carry.

## Second orbit — the connections that could change the answer

### The Unresolved Relationship
A relationship is not automatically a discovery. It is a question with a shape.

Follow a proposed connection and keep its assumptions visible. What would strengthen it? What would break it? Is the link causal, temporal, structural, metaphorical, or only a resemblance?

Enter through [The Between in the companion repository](https://github.com/MirrorCartographer/mirror-cartographer-ui/tree/main/research-atlas).

### The Contradiction Laboratory
When two claims disagree, do not flatten one to preserve a clean story. Find the conditions under which each appears true.

Ask: are they measuring different things, at different times, at different scales, under different constraints? Is one a direct observation and the other an interpretation? What evidence would resolve the disagreement?

Follow [evidence](evidence/), [ledgers](ledgers/), and [proof](proof/).

### The Research Autopsy
Take a confident conclusion apart. Find its source, inference steps, hidden assumptions, omitted alternatives, and unsupported leaps. Then reconstruct only what survives.

A failed hypothesis is not a failed investigation. It can be the first honest map of the terrain.

Start with [research](research/) and [demonstrations](demonstrations/).

## Third orbit — biology, healing, and time

These are research directions, not claims that the project has discovered cures.

### The Biological Digital Twin
How could separate signals become a time-aware representation of a changing biological system—without mistaking a model for the person?

The hard problem is not merely collecting more measurements. It is aligning timescales, separating stable traits from transient states, accounting for missing observations, and recording which predictions are validated.

Explore [mechanistic models](research/mechanistic_models/) and [hypothesis tests](research/hypothesis-tests/).

### The Healing Possibility Mapper
Instead of asking only what a disease is called, ask which mechanisms are active, which remain modifiable, which interventions target them, and what evidence would show a meaningful response.

For neurodegenerative questions, the essential distinctions include injury versus dysfunction, progression versus fluctuation, protection versus regeneration, and a plausible mechanism versus demonstrated clinical benefit.

Enter through [cure discovery](research/cure_discovery/) and [frontier discovery](research/frontier-discovery/). Treat every proposed connection as a candidate for testing, not a treatment recommendation.

### The Scientific Time Machine
Reconstruct how understanding changed: what was known at the time, what new observation arrived, which interpretation shifted, and why.

The time machine is a provenance trail, not a claim that history can be replayed perfectly. Its essential sequence is:

**what happened → what was observed → what was inferred → what was proposed → what changed → why it changed → what contradicted it → what evidence supports it now → what remains unknown**

## Fourth orbit — systems that fail people

A system can be logically consistent and still fail at the point where a person must use it.

The veteran-welfare question is a particularly clear example: eligibility on paper is not access in practice. Between the rule and the outcome sit documents, deadlines, interfaces, staffing, interpretation, health, money, and the energy required to persist.

The same structural question applies to research administration, healthcare, disability support, and software: **where does the intended path break, and who bears the cost of the break?**

Look through [governance](governance/), [decision support](mind/decision-support/), and [care](mind/care/).

## Fifth orbit — the mind that keeps the thread

A discovery loses force when its context disappears. Preserve the path that made it meaningful: sources, counterexamples, decisions, abandoned routes, and the exact reason a conclusion changed.

The goal is not to save every fragment forever. It is to make important transformations recoverable.

Visit [mind](mind/), [continuity](continuity/), and [source index](source-index/).

## Sixth orbit — the creative life of the method

Not every question should begin as a table. Some begin as an image, a sound, a room, a sentence, or a small impossible world.

The creative layer is not decoration on top of the research. It can make a structure perceptible before the right technical vocabulary exists. But metaphor must remain distinguishable from evidence.

## How to follow an obsession without losing the plot

For any compelling idea, leave five marks:

1. **The spark:** what made this worth following?
2. **The shape:** what is the relationship or mechanism being proposed?
3. **The receipts:** which sources or observations support it?
4. **The resistance:** what contradicts it or remains unexplained?
5. **The next move:** what observation, test, conversation, or build could change the state of knowledge?

Keep a distinction between *emotionally resonant*, *conceptually promising*, *empirically supported*, and *demonstrated in working software*. Each can matter; they are not interchangeable.

## The return point

When the repository starts to feel like a pile of files, return to the original question:

**What is visible? What is missing? What would change the answer?**

The point is not to make everything connect. It is to make meaningful connections inspectable—and to let the real ones survive.
